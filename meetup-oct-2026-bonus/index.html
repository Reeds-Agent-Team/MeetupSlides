How to read it
The goal is a warm house
You were going to buy that heat anyway. The miner is a resistance heater that turns electricity into heat 1:1, and on your own power it makes the heat cheaper than oil. The sats it returns are the discount, not the business.
Sats column
The savings converted at today's price ($83,904). Only the 307k sats are actually mined. The rest is oil or gas money that stays in your pocket, which you can stack if you like.
Bonus · no-KYC
The mined sats carry no ID and no premium. Buying them without KYC would cost 5–10% over spot, about $275 for the same 307k sats.

Case Study · Part 3 of 3 · Decision guide for Western Mass bitcoiners
When does mining for heat make sense for you?
No
Grid power, any fuel
$0.247/ kWh heat
At $0.31–0.36 power the sats cover a fifth of the bill. You're paying $1.65/day more than oil and $3.95 more than gas to heat with hashrate. Fine as a hobby or a cold-snap backup, not as a heating plan.
Only on solar
You heat with natural gas
$0.11/ kWh to beat
Gas is the cheapest heat in the valley. The miner only wins below ~$0.18 power, i.e. on your own panels or surplus credits. The upside is smaller (~$0.03–0.06/kWh saved) but the sats are the same.
Yes
Oil or propane + solar surplus
−$0.067/ kWh heat
Credits you'd never use become 79 sats and 1 kWh of heat each. Beats oil at any price down to ~$3.50/gal. This is the sweet spot for most of the hilltowns and the valley: oil boiler, south-facing roof, long winter.
Yes, with a cap
Buying panels for it
$0.047/ kWh heat
Own-roof power at $0.11–0.14 minus the subsidy beats oil and gas. But only for kWh inside your annual production. Every kWh past that is bought from Eversource at $0.314 and the case flips.
The one rule · size the miner to your credit bank, not your sunshine
  • Net metering is 1:1, so it doesn't matter whether the miner runs at noon or midnight. Only the annual balance matters: production − house load = the kWh you can mine for free.
  • Worked example: 14 kW roof, ~15,000 kWh/yr, house uses ~10,400 → ~4,300 kWh surplus. One S19j Pro at 1.2 kW × 18 h × 180 days = 3,900 kWh. Fits, with ~400 kWh to spare. A second miner, or a heat-pump water heater, does not.
  • Overshoot and the miner is suddenly on $0.314 grid power, losing $1.65/day vs oil. Watch the Eversource credit balance each bill and throttle runtime with the firmware power target, not the breaker.
  • Hardware: used S19j Pro ~$100–150, APW12 120 V mod (two resistors), Braiins or LuxOS, 14 AWG cords, dedicated 15 A circuit, smart plug with overcurrent cutoff. Point the exhaust into open basement, not a wall.
  • The optimistic case: a cold winter stacks ~300k sats of no-KYC bitcoin per miner while the oil truck comes less often. If bitcoin does what we think it does, the heat was free and the sats were the cheapest you'll ever get.
  • Get a baseline first: oil fill history, a clamp on the boiler circuit, a temp sensor near the miner. Measure, then decide.
All figures Oct. 2026, Hadley MA. Assumes a $0 value for surplus credits, $0.19/kWh oil heat, $0.11/kWh gas heat, 47.6 sats/TH/day hashprice, 25 yr panel life with the $1,000 MA credit and no federal credit. Not financial advice; your roof, your boiler, your sats.
